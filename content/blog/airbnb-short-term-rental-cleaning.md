Cleanliness is the single most mentioned factor in short-term rental reviews, and it's the one thing a guest notices in the first thirty seconds. For Louisville hosts juggling back-to-back bookings, a reliable turnover process is the difference between five-star ratings and a quiet listing.

## Why turnovers are their own kind of clean

A short-term rental clean isn't the same as tidying your own home. Every turnover has to reset the property to a hotel-fresh standard for a guest who has never seen it before. That means no trace of the last visitor, no missed details, and no shortcuts, all on a tight clock between checkout and the next check-in.

The pressure is real: you usually get one window, often just a few hours on the same day. Miss it, and a guest arrives to an unready space, which is exactly the experience that earns a bad review.

## The turnover checklist that protects your reviews

A consistent room-by-room routine keeps every stay identical. A complete turnover should cover:

- **Kitchen** — wipe all surfaces, run and empty the dishwasher, clean the sink and appliances, check for expired or left-behind food
- **Bathrooms** — scrub the toilet, tub, and shower, descale fixtures, replace towels, restock toilet paper and toiletries
- **Bedrooms** — strip and remake beds with fresh linens, check under beds and in closets for forgotten items
- **Living areas** — dust, vacuum, fluff cushions, reset the space to its staged layout
- **Restock and reset** — coffee, paper goods, soap, and anything else your listing promises
- **Final walkthrough** — look at the space the way a guest will, from the entry inward

The restocking and linen reset are what separate a true turnover from a basic clean. Guests expect the property to feel brand new, every time.
