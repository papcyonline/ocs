People often use "commercial cleaning" and "janitorial services" as if they mean the same thing. They overlap, but they are not identical, and knowing the difference helps you buy the right service for your facility instead of paying for the wrong scope.

## The short version

Think of it as routine versus deep.

**Janitorial services** cover the recurring, day-to-day tasks that keep a building functional and presentable. This is the steady upkeep that happens daily or several times a week.

**Commercial cleaning** is a broader term that usually points to the larger, deeper, or more specialized jobs. These happen on a periodic schedule or as one-time projects, and they often require equipment and methods beyond a typical daily routine.

Both are about keeping your space clean. The difference is mostly scale, frequency, and the kind of work involved.

## What janitorial work looks like

Janitorial service is the backbone of a clean facility. It is the maintenance that keeps things from sliding day to day:

- Emptying trash and recycling
- Vacuuming carpets and mopping hard floors
- Cleaning and sanitizing restrooms
- Restocking soap, paper towels, and toilet paper
- Wiping down common areas, break rooms, and high-touch surfaces
- Spot-cleaning glass and entryways

This work is repetitive by design. Its value is in consistency, so your team and visitors never walk into a stale break room or an out-of-supplies restroom.

## What commercial cleaning covers

Commercial cleaning handles the heavier lifting that routine janitorial work is not built for. These jobs tend to be periodic, project-based, or specialized:

- Strip and wax of hard floors to restore shine and protection
- Pressure washing of exterior walkways, entrances, and surfaces
- Post-construction cleanup after a build-out or renovation
- Deep restroom sanitizing beyond daily maintenance
- Larger-scale office cleaning across a full facility

These tasks often need specific equipment and a crew that knows how to do them safely and well. You would not expect them every night, but skipping them entirely lets a building wear down faster than it should.

## How they work together

A well-maintained facility usually needs both, working on different rhythms.

| | Janitorial services | Commercial cleaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Frequency | Daily or several times a week | Periodic or project-based |
| Focus | Routine upkeep and supplies | Deep, specialized, or large-scale work |
| Examples | Trash, restrooms, vacuuming | Strip and wax, pressure washing, post-construction |

The daily janitorial routine keeps the building clean between the bigger jobs, and the periodic commercial work resets surfaces that ordinary cleaning cannot fully restore. Run only one and the gaps show: skip janitorial and the place feels neglected fast, skip the deep work and floors and exteriors slowly dull.
