Stainless steel gives a kitchen a clean, modern look — right up until the first round of fingerprints, water spots, and streaks show up. The metal isn't hard to clean, but it does have a few rules. Follow them and you'll get an even, smudge-free shine every time.

## Find the grain

This is the single most important step, and most people skip it. Stainless steel has a directional grain — fine lines running either horizontally or vertically across each surface. Cleaning with the grain lifts dirt out of those lines; cleaning against it pushes grime in and leaves streaks. Look closely at each panel, since the grain can run differently on the door versus the handle, and always wipe in that direction.

## Everyday cleaning

For routine smudges and fingerprints, you rarely need anything special:

1. Mix warm water with a drop of dish soap.
2. Dampen a soft microfiber cloth — not soaking — and wipe along the grain.
3. Rinse the cloth and go over the surface again with clean water to remove any soap.
4. Immediately buff dry with a second, clean microfiber cloth, following the grain.

That final dry buff is what eliminates streaks. Leaving any moisture or cleaner to air-dry is what causes them in the first place.

## For grease and stuck-on grime

Stovetops, oven doors, and the area around handles collect cooking grease that water alone won't cut. A lightly diluted white vinegar spray works well: mist it on, wipe along the grain, then rinse with a damp cloth and buff dry. Use it sparingly and don't let it sit, since stainless steel prefers gentle treatment.

## Bring back the shine

Once the surface is clean and dry, a touch of oil restores that showroom luster and helps repel future fingerprints:

- Put a few drops of food-safe mineral oil (or a dedicated stainless cleaner) on a clean microfiber cloth.
- Wipe a thin, even layer along the grain.
- Buff with a dry cloth until there's no oily residue, just shine.

A little goes a long way — too much oil attracts dust and smudges faster.

## What to avoid

A few things will scratch or dull the finish for good:

- **Abrasive pads, steel wool, or scouring powders** that leave permanent scratches
- **Bleach and ammonia-based cleaners**, which can stain and corrode
- **Wiping against the grain**, which traps grime and creates streaks
- **Letting water spots air-dry**, especially with hard water

## Keep it looking new

Stainless steel stays sharp with small, regular habits: a quick wipe of handles and high-touch spots, drying after splashes, and a full clean-and-buff every week or two.
