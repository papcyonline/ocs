There is no single right answer to how often an office should be cleaned. The honest answer is: it depends on your space, your people, and how hard the office gets used. But you can land on the right frequency by looking at a few clear factors.

## What drives the right frequency

A few things determine how often your office really needs attention:

- **Foot traffic.** More people moving through the space means more dirt, more germs, and more wear. A busy lobby needs more than a quiet back office.
- **Team size.** More employees means more shared surfaces, more restroom use, and more break room activity.
- **Type of work.** A clinic, a food-adjacent business, or a customer-facing showroom has higher standards than a small back-office operation.
- **Visibility to clients.** If customers and visitors regularly walk through, appearances carry more weight.

Weigh these together and the picture gets clearer. A high-traffic, client-facing office sits at the frequent end, while a small, low-traffic team can stretch the schedule further.

## A simple frequency guide

Here is a practical starting point for most workspaces:

| Office type | Suggested frequency |
| --- | --- |
| High-traffic or client-facing | Daily or several times a week |
| Mid-size standard office | Two to three times a week |
| Small or low-traffic office | Weekly, plus periodic deep cleaning |

Treat this as a baseline, not a rule. The right schedule is the one that keeps your space consistently presentable without paying for visits you do not need.

## Some things need attention more often than others

Frequency is not one-size-fits-all even within a single office. Certain areas pile up faster and should be cleaned more often than the rest of the space:

- **Restrooms** need frequent sanitizing and restocking, often daily, regardless of overall office size.
- **Break rooms and kitchens** collect spills, crumbs, and odors quickly wherever food is involved.
- **High-touch surfaces** like door handles, shared keyboards, and elevator buttons spread germs and deserve regular disinfection.

Meanwhile, deeper tasks like floor strip and wax or detailed deep cleaning happen on a longer cycle. A smart schedule layers frequent upkeep of the busy spots with periodic deeper work.

## Why consistency matters more than intensity

It is tempting to skip regular service and just book an occasional big clean. The problem is that an office slides between those cleans, and your team spends the in-between days in a space that feels neglected.

A consistent schedule keeps standards steady, which is exactly what supports focus, health, and morale. The point of choosing the right frequency is to make "clean" your normal, not a once-in-a-while reset.
