When you move out of a rental, your security deposit often comes down to one thing: **how clean you leave the place.** Landlords and property managers inspect against a standard, and "broom clean" rarely cuts it. Here's the room-by-room checklist they actually use — and where renters most often lose money.

## Kitchen (the #1 deposit battleground)

The kitchen is inspected harder than any other room. Don't skip:

- **Inside the oven** — baked-on grease is the most common deduction
- **Inside and behind the refrigerator** — pull it out and clean underneath
- **Inside all cabinets and drawers** — crumbs, liners, spills
- **Range hood, stovetop, and backsplash**
- **Sink, faucet, and garbage disposal**
- **Countertops and the floor, including corners and edges**

## Bathrooms

- **Toilet** — bowl, base, and behind it
- **Tub and shower** — scrub grout, remove soap scum, descale the showerhead
- **Sink, vanity, and mirror**
- **Exhaust fan cover and light fixtures**
- **Floor, baseboards, and behind the door**

## Bedrooms, living areas, and hallways

- **Closets** — shelves, rods, and floors, fully emptied
- **Baseboards, door frames, and window sills**
- **Windows, tracks, and blinds**
- **Light fixtures, ceiling fans, and vents**
- **Walls** — spot-clean scuffs and any adhesive marks
- **Floors** — vacuum carpet thoroughly; mop hard floors

## The details that trigger deductions

These small items are exactly what an inspector looks for:

- Dust on the tops of doors and ceiling fan blades
- Fingerprints around light switches and handles
- Cobwebs in ceiling corners
- A dirty washer/dryer if they convey with the unit
- Marks left behind by wall hangings

## A few tips that protect your deposit

1. **Read your lease first.** Many specify "professionally cleaned" condition — meet that standard exactly.
2. **Clean after everything is out.** You can't clean a closet floor with boxes in it.
3. **Take date-stamped photos** of every room when you're done. They're your proof in a dispute.
4. **Keep the receipt** if you hire a pro — it documents that the standard was met.
