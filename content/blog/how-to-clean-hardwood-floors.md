Hardwood floors add warmth and value to a home, but they're easy to ruin with the wrong routine. Too much water, an acidic cleaner, or a gritty mop can dull a finish that should last decades. Done right, cleaning hardwood is quick and keeps the wood looking its best.

## Know your finish first

Most modern floors are sealed with a surface finish like polyurethane, which sits on top of the wood and handles light cleaning well. Older or specialty floors may have a penetrating oil or wax finish that needs different care and no water. If you're unsure, test a small, hidden area before using any cleaner — and when in doubt, keep moisture to an absolute minimum.

## Dry cleaning is your daily defense

The biggest threat to hardwood isn't dirt — it's grit. Tiny particles of sand and debris act like sandpaper underfoot, scratching the finish a little more every day. Staying ahead of it is simple:

- **Sweep or dust-mop** high-traffic areas several times a week with a soft-bristle broom or microfiber pad.
- **Vacuum** with a hard-floor setting or a brush attachment, not a beater bar that can scuff the surface.
- **Use mats** at entrances and a no-shoes rule to keep grit and moisture from coming in.

## Damp-mop the right way

When floors need more than dusting, less water is always better. Here's the safe routine:

1. Sweep or vacuum first so you're not dragging grit around.
2. Use a cleaner formulated for sealed hardwood, or a tiny amount of mild soap in water — nothing acidic.
3. Wring your mop or cloth until it's barely damp. It should feel almost dry to the touch.
4. Mop along the grain in sections, rinsing as you go.
5. Dry any lingering moisture promptly with a clean cloth so nothing pools in the seams.

Avoid soaking the floor, and never let water sit. If you spill, wipe it up right away.

## What never to use

A few common products do real damage to wood:

- **Vinegar and ammonia** are acidic enough to dull the finish over time.
- **Oil soaps and wax** can leave residue or buildup that's hard to remove.
- **Steam mops** force heat and moisture into the wood and seams.
- **Abrasive pads and scrubbers** scratch the surface.

## Protect the finish long-term

A little prevention keeps the wood looking new for years. Put felt pads under furniture legs, lift rather than drag heavy items, trim pet nails, and wipe up spills immediately. Area rugs in busy spots take the brunt of the wear, and rotating them occasionally evens out fading from sunlight.
