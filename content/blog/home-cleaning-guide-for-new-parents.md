Bringing a newborn home rearranges your whole world — and your cleaning routine along with it. You want a safe, hygienic space for your baby, but you're also running on very little sleep. The goal isn't a spotless house; it's a clean, healthy one that you can maintain without burning out. Here's how to focus your energy where it counts.

## Clean, not sterile

First, give yourself permission to relax. A baby's home needs to be hygienic, not operating-room sterile. In fact, a reasonably clean environment helps a baby's immune system develop normally. So aim for consistent, sensible cleaning of the things that matter most, and let go of the pressure to scrub every surface daily.

## Choose baby-safe products

A newborn's lungs and skin are far more sensitive than an adult's, and babies spend their days close to floors and surfaces. Swap harsh cleaners for gentler options:

- Use fragrance-free, plant-based cleaners wherever possible.
- Avoid bleach, ammonia, and heavily scented sprays that linger in the air.
- Ventilate rooms while you clean and let surfaces dry fully before the baby returns.
- Keep all cleaning products stored well out of reach.

This is the same standard our crews hold to — eco-friendly, pet- and kid-safe supplies on every visit — because the littlest members of a household are the most vulnerable to what's in the air.

## Prioritize the high-contact zones

When time and energy are scarce, concentrate on what your baby actually touches:

1. **The nursery:** Dust surfaces, wipe the crib and changing table, and wash crib sheets and blankets in a gentle, fragrance-free detergent.
2. **Feeding gear:** Wash bottles, pump parts, and pacifiers thoroughly after each use.
3. **High-touch surfaces:** Wipe doorknobs, light switches, faucet handles, and phones, which collect germs from every visitor.
4. **Floors:** Once your baby starts rolling and crawling, clean floors are essential. Damp-mop hard floors and vacuum rugs regularly.

Everything else can wait. A pile of laundry never hurt a baby; harsh fumes can.

## Keep the air clean

Indoor air quality matters more than most new parents realize. Newborns breathe faster than adults, so airborne irritants reach them more readily. Keep the air fresh by skipping aerosol sprays and plug-in fresheners, opening windows when weather allows, and changing your HVAC filter on schedule. ## Build a routine you can actually keep

Forget marathon cleaning sessions — they're not realistic right now. Instead, lean on tiny, repeatable habits:

- A two-minute wipe-down of the changing area after diaper changes
- A quick nightly tidy of bottles and feeding supplies
- One small task per day rather than a weekend overhaul
- A standing list on the fridge so visiting family can pitch in

Small and consistent wins every time, especially when you're exhausted.
